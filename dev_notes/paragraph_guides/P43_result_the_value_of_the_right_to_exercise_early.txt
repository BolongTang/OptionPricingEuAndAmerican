Maps the early-exercise premium over spot, volatility, rate and maturity, so the reader can say when
American pricing matters.

EEP = American minus European on the same lattice, so the lattice error cancels. Figures eepcurves

  S3-S4  [2 sentences]  FOCUS: Baseline: at the money the premium is (macro EEPatm), or (macro EEPatmPct) percent of the European put; it is largest deep in the money and for high rates and low volatility (Figure eepcurves).

  S7-S8  [2 sentences]  FOCUS: Maturity: at the money the premium grows with the maturity (Figure eepcurves, right panel); parity bounds for American options: S - K <= C - P_A <= S - K e^{-rT}; here (macro ParityLo) <= (macro ParityMid) <= (macro ParityHi).

DRAFT SENTENCES CURRENTLY IN THE TEX FILE (placeholders to rewrite in your own words, one per line)
  S1. The early-exercise premium is the difference between the American and the European value
      computed on the same lattice, so that the lattice error cancels and the premium is exactly
      zero when there is no early exercise.
  S2. It is zero for $r=0$ and for a call without dividends.
  S3. At the money in the baseline the premium is \EEPatm, which is \EEPatmPct\% of the European
      put.
  S4. Figure~\ref{fig:eepcurves} shows that it grows as the put goes deeper into the money and with
      higher interest rates and lower volatility, which are the conditions under which the holder
      wants to collect the strike early.
  S5. The map in Figure~\ref{fig:eepmap} shows the premium in price units and as a percentage of the
      European put.
  S6. Over the region plotted, spot $6$ to $14$ and volatility $10\%$ to $50\%$, the relative
      premium reaches about \EEPmaxPct\% of the European value.
  S7. The premium at the money also grows with the maturity, as the right panel of
      Figure~\ref{fig:eepcurves} shows.
  S8. For American options put--call parity becomes the inequality $S_0-K\le C-P_A\le S_0-Ke^{-rT}$,
      and the numbers are $\ParityLo\le\ParityMid\le\ParityHi$, so the American put respects the
      bounds.
