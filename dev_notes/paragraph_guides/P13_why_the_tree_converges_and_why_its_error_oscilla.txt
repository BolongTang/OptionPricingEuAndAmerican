ROLE IN THE ARGUMENT
Explains the order of convergence and the saw-tooth, because the numerical tables of Section 4.4
cannot be understood without them.

CONTEXT THE READER ALREADY HAS / WHAT YOU MUST ESTABLISH
The reader has the tree. They know the central limit theorem and that a call payoff has a kink at
the strike.

SENTENCE PLAN (write in this order; the numbers in brackets are sentence counts)
  S1-S2  [2 sentences]  FOCUS: Convergence to GBM: ln S_N is a sum of N independent steps +/- sigma sqrt(dt) with drift, so by the CLT it tends to the normal law with mean ln S0 + (r - sigma^2/2)T and variance sigma^2 T, i.e. the GBM law.
  S3-S4  [2 sentences]  FOCUS: Order of convergence: the error is of order 1/N, i.e. first order, but not monotone: it carries a factor (-1)^N because of the kink.
  S5-S6  [2 sentences]  FOCUS: Four remedies: averaging N and N+1; Broadie-Detemple smoothing (replace the last layer by the Black-Scholes value of one step); Richardson extrapolation 2V_{2N} - V_N; the Leisen-Reimer tree, centred on the strike, of order 1/N^2.
  S7  [1 sentence]  FOCUS: Why it matters for the rest of the report: early exercise needs a lattice, so the plain CRR tree is kept for Part C and the accelerations are used only where exercise is European.

DRAFT SENTENCES CURRENTLY IN THE TEX FILE (placeholders to rewrite in your own words, one per line)
  S1. The terminal log-price of the tree is $\ln S_0+\sigma\sqrt{\Delta t}\,(2B-N)$ with
      $B\sim\mathrm{Bin}(N,p)$, a sum of $N$ independent steps.
  S2. By the central limit theorem it converges to a normal law with mean $\ln
      S_0+(r-\tfrac12\sigma^2)T$ and variance $\sigma^2T$, which is the law of $\ln S_T$ under
      geometric Brownian motion, so the tree price converges to the Black--Scholes price.
  S3. The error of the tree price is of order $1/N$, but it is not monotone in $N$.
  S4. With $K=S_0$ a terminal node sits exactly at the strike when $N$ is even and the kink falls
      half-way between two nodes when $N$ is odd, so the error behaves like $(-1)^Nc/N$ rather than
      $c/N$.
  S5. Four standard remedies are compared in Section~\ref{sec:B-quality}: averaging the prices for
      $N$ and $N+1$ steps; Broadie--Detemple smoothing, which replaces the last layer by the Black--
      Scholes value of the one-step claim; Richardson extrapolation $2V_{2N}-V_N$; and the Leisen--
      Reimer tree, which is built so that the strike coincides with a node boundary.
  S6. The first three remove the oscillation and the Leisen--Reimer tree additionally raises the
      order of convergence from one to two.
  S7. Accelerations that use a closed form at the last step apply only to European payoffs, so for
      the American put of Part C we keep the plain CRR lattice and control the error by refinement
      and extrapolation.

DERIVATION / LATEX MATERIAL (use as the skeleton of the displayed equations)
Terminal log price: $\ln S_N=\ln S_0+\sigma\sqrt{\Delta t}\,(2B-N)$, $B\sim\mathrm{Bin}(N,p)$.
Mean: $N\sigma\sqrt{\Delta t}(2p-1)\to(r-\tfrac12\sigma^2)T$ because $2p-1=\dfrac{(r-\frac12\sigma^2)\sqrt{\Delta t}}{\sigma}+o(\sqrt{\Delta t})$; variance $\to\sigma^2T$.
Leisen-Reimer: $p=h(d_2)$, $p^*=h(d_1)$ with the Peizer-Pratt inversion $h$; $u=e^{r\Delta t}p^*/p$, $d=(e^{r\Delta t}-pu)/(1-p)$.
