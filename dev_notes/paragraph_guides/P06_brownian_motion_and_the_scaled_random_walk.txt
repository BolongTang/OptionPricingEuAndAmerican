DRAFT SENTENCES CURRENTLY IN THE TEX FILE (placeholders to rewrite in your own words, one per line)
  S1. A process $W=\{W_t\}_{t\ge0}$ is a Brownian motion on $(\Omega,\mathcal F,\{\mathcal
      F_t\},\mathbb P)$ if it is adapted with $W_0=0$, if $W_t-W_s\sim N(0,t-s)$ for $0\le s\le t$,
      if $W_t-W_s$ is independent of $\mathcal F_s$, and if $t\mapsto W_t(\omega)$ is continuous.
  S2. Independence and the Gaussian law of increments fix all finite-dimensional distributions, and
      continuity of the paths is what separates Brownian motion from other processes with the same
      increments.
  S3. The scaled symmetric random walk replaces the Gaussian increments by coin flips: with
      $\xi_i=\pm1$ equally likely,
  S4. \begin{equation}\label{eq:scaledwalk}  W^{(N)}_{t_k}=\sqrt{T/N}\sum_{i=1}^{k}\xi_i,\qquad
      t_k=kT/N, \end{equation} so that $\mathbb E\,W^{(N)}_{t_k}=0$ and
      $\operatorname{Var}W^{(N)}_{t_k}=t_k$ for every $N$.
  S5. Mean, variance and independence of increments therefore agree with Brownian motion for every
      $N$, whereas the lattice support and the non-Gaussian increments do not.
  S6. Donsker's theorem states that the whole path $W^{(N)}$ converges in law to $W$ as
      $N\to\infty$, which is the statement we test numerically.
  S7. This matters for pricing because the CRR tree of Section~\ref{sec:B} is exactly such a walk
      with a drift, so its convergence to geometric Brownian motion is a consequence of the
      convergence studied here.

DERIVATION / LATEX MATERIAL (use as the skeleton of the displayed equations)
Mean/variance of the walk: $\mathbb E[\xi_i]=0$, $\operatorname{Var}\xi_i=1$, independence $\Rightarrow \operatorname{Var}W^{(N)}_{t_k}=\frac TN k=t_k$.
Connection to the tree: log-price $\ln S_{t_k}=\ln S_0+\sigma\sqrt{\Delta t}\sum \xi_i+(\text{drift})$ in the CRR tree.
