ROLE IN THE ARGUMENT
Derives the PDE satisfied by the option price from a hedging argument, the continuous-time
counterpart of the one-step replication of the tree.

CONTEXT THE READER ALREADY HAS / WHAT YOU MUST ESTABLISH
The reader has Ito's formula and the idea of a self-financing portfolio.

SENTENCE PLAN (write in this order; the numbers in brackets are sentence counts)
  S1-S2  [2 sentences]  FOCUS: Set-up: the option value V(t,S_t) and a portfolio short one option and long Delta shares; the value of the portfolio is Pi = Delta S - V.
  S3-S4  [2 sentences]  FOCUS: It\^o gives dV = V_t dt + V_S dS + (1/2) V_SS sigma^2 S^2 dt; choosing Delta = V_S removes the dW terms.
  S5-S6  [2 sentences]  FOCUS: No arbitrage: a riskless portfolio must earn the riskless rate, d Pi = r Pi dt = r(S V_S - V) dt; equating gives the PDE; display.
  S7-S8  [2 sentences]  FOCUS: The drift mu has disappeared; the terminal condition V(T,S) = (S - K)^+ selects the call; the Feynman-Kac formula says that the solution is the discounted Q-expectation of Section 2.

DRAFT SENTENCES CURRENTLY IN THE TEX FILE (placeholders to rewrite in your own words, one per line)
  S1. Let $V(t,S_t)$ be the value of the option and consider a portfolio that is short one option
      and long $\Delta$ shares, so that $\Pi=\Delta S-V$.
  S2. We choose $\Delta$ so that the portfolio has no exposure to the Brownian motion.
  S3. It\^o's formula gives $dV=V_t\,dt+V_S\,dS+\tfrac12\sigma^2S^2V_{SS}\,dt$ under the real-world
      dynamics~\eqref{eq:gbm}.
  S4. With $\Delta=V_S$ the terms in $dS$ cancel in $d\Pi=\Delta\,dS-dV$, which leaves the
      deterministic increment $d\Pi=-\big(V_t+\tfrac12\sigma^2S^2V_{SS}\big)\,dt$.
  S5. A portfolio without risk must earn the riskless rate, so $d\Pi=r\Pi\,dt=r(SV_S-V)\,dt$.
  S6. Equating the two expressions gives the Black--Scholes PDE \begin{equation}\label{eq:bspde}
      V_t+rSV_S+\tfrac12\sigma^2S^2V_{SS}-rV=0 . \end{equation}
  S7. The real-world drift $\mu$ has disappeared from the equation, which is the continuous-time
      form of the statement that the price does not depend on $\mu$.
  S8. With the terminal condition $V(T,S)=(S-K)^+$ the solution is, by the Feynman--Kac formula, the
      discounted risk-neutral expectation $e^{-r(T-t)}\mathbb E^{\mathbb Q}[(S_T-K)^+\mid S_t=S]$ of
      Section~\ref{sec:setting}.

FIGURES / TABLES / EQUATIONS TO REFER TO
Equations eq:gbm, eq:bspde.

DERIVATION / LATEX MATERIAL (use as the skeleton of the displayed equations)
$d\Pi=\Delta\,dS-dV=(\Delta-V_S)\,dS-(V_t+\tfrac12\sigma^2S^2V_{SS})\,dt$. With $\Delta=V_S$ the first term vanishes.
Equate: $-(V_t+\tfrac12\sigma^2S^2V_{SS})=r(SV_S-V)\Rightarrow V_t+rSV_S+\tfrac12\sigma^2S^2V_{SS}-rV=0$.
Greeks form: $\Theta+rS\Delta+\tfrac12\sigma^2S^2\Gamma=rV$.
