ROLE IN THE ARGUMENT
Closes the loop between the two descriptions of the American put: the tree is an explicit finite-
difference scheme for the variational inequality.

CONTEXT THE READER ALREADY HAS / WHAT YOU MUST ESTABLISH
The reader has both objects. They know Taylor expansion and the expansion of p from Section 4.1.

SENTENCE PLAN (write in this order; the numbers in brackets are sentence counts)
  S1-S2  [2 sentences]  FOCUS: Idea: expand one step of the recursion in the continuation region and show that it reproduces (L - r)V = 0 as dt tends to 0.
  S3-S4  [2 sentences]  FOCUS: Taylor expansions of V(t + dt, x u) and V(t + dt, x d) to order dt, with x u - x = x(y + y^2/2 + ...); display.
  S5-S6  [2 sentences]  FOCUS: Probability: from p u + (1-p) d = e^{r dt} one gets 2p - 1 = (r - sigma^2/2) sqrt(dt)/sigma + o(sqrt dt); the sqrt(dt)-terms then combine to (r - sigma^2/2) x dt V_x, and the (1/2) sigma^2 dt x V_x terms complete r x V_x dt.
  S7-S8  [2 sentences]  FOCUS: Conclusion: multiplying by e^{-r dt} = 1 - r dt + ... and requiring the right-hand side of the recursion to equal V gives (L - r)V = 0; where exercise is better the max sets V = K - x; complementarity is automatic, so the tree is an explicit finite-difference scheme for the variational inequality.

DRAFT SENTENCES CURRENTLY IN THE TEX FILE (placeholders to rewrite in your own words, one per line)
  S1. We show that, in the continuation region, one step of the recursion reproduces the PDE as
      $\Delta t\to0$.
  S2. Write $y=\sigma\sqrt{\Delta t}$ so that $u=e^y$ and expand the value function at the two
      successor nodes.
  S3. A Taylor expansion to order $\Delta t$ gives the two successor values
  S4. \begin{align} V(t+\Delta t,xu)&=V+V_t\Delta t+V_x\,x\big(\sigma\sqrt{\Delta
      t}+\tfrac12\sigma^2\Delta t\big)+\tfrac12V_{xx}x^2\sigma^2\Delta t+\dots\\ V(t+\Delta
      t,xd)&=V+V_t\Delta t+V_x\,x\big(-\sigma\sqrt{\Delta t}+\tfrac12\sigma^2\Delta
      t\big)+\tfrac12V_{xx}x^2\sigma^2\Delta t+\dots \end{align}
  S5. From $pu+(1-p)d=e^{r\Delta t}$ one obtains $2p-1=\frac{(r-\frac12\sigma^2)\sqrt{\Delta
      t}}{\sigma}+o(\sqrt{\Delta t})$.
  S6. Hence the $\sqrt{\Delta t}$-terms contribute $(r-\tfrac12\sigma^2)x\,\Delta t\,V_x$, the
      $\tfrac12\sigma^2\Delta t\,xV_x$ terms complete this to $rxV_x\Delta t$, and
      $pV_u+(1-p)V_d=V+\big(V_t+rxV_x+\tfrac12\sigma^2x^2V_{xx}\big)\Delta t+o(\Delta t)$.
  S7. Multiplying by $e^{-r\Delta t}=1-r\Delta t+\dots$ and requiring the right-hand side of
      Equation~\eqref{eq:crr} to equal $V$ gives $(\mathcal L-r)V=0$ in the continuation region,
      while in the stopping region the maximum sets $V=K-x$.
  S8. Complementarity is therefore automatic, the tree is an explicit finite-difference scheme for
      the variational inequality~\eqref{eq:VI}, and both give the same price as $M_{bin}\to\infty$.

FIGURES / TABLES / EQUATIONS TO REFER TO
Equations eq:crr, eq:VI.

DERIVATION / LATEX MATERIAL (use as the skeleton of the displayed equations)
$pV_u+(1-p)V_d=V+V_t\Delta t+V_xx\Big[(p-(1-p))\sigma\sqrt{\Delta t}+\tfrac12\sigma^2\Delta t\Big]+\tfrac12V_{xx}x^2\sigma^2\Delta t+o(\Delta t)$.
$(2p-1)\sigma\sqrt{\Delta t}=(r-\tfrac12\sigma^2)\Delta t+o(\Delta t)$, so the bracket equals $r\Delta t$.
Requirement $V=e^{-r\Delta t}(pV_u+(1-p)V_d)$ $\Rightarrow$ $0=V_t+rxV_x+\tfrac12\sigma^2x^2V_{xx}-rV$ at order $\Delta t$.

PITFALLS / DO NOT SAY
'o(dt)' hides terms of order dt^{3/2} that cancel by symmetry of the lattice; do not claim a
convergence rate from this expansion alone.
