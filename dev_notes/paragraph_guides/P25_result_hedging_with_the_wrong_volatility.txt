ROLE IN THE ARGUMENT
Shows the single most important practical risk of the Black-Scholes hedge and ties the numbers to a
closed-form identity.

CONTEXT THE READER ALREADY HAS / WHAT YOU MUST ESTABLISH
The seller prices and hedges at the historical volatility 37.7 percent, but the stock realises
another volatility. Table tbl:hedgewrong lists simulated mean P&L next to the gamma formula, 10,000
paths with daily rebalancing.

SENTENCE PLAN (write in this order; the numbers in brackets are sentence counts)
  S1-S2  [2 sentences]  FOCUS: The identity: to leading order the seller's discounted P&L is the integral over time of e^{-rt} (1/2) Gamma S^2 (sigma_h^2 - sigma_real^2); the seller loses when realised variance exceeds the variance used to hedge.
  S3-S4  [2 sentences]  FOCUS: Numerical check: at sigma_real = 55 percent the simulated mean P&L is \WrongVolLoss dollars and the formula gives \WrongVolTheory dollars.
  S5-S6  [2 sentences]  FOCUS: Symmetry: at lower realised volatility (25 and 30 percent) the seller gains by about the same amounts; the P&L is nearly linear in the variance gap.
  S7  [1 sentence]  FOCUS: Bridge to Part C: the mis-specified-volatility experiment for the American put has the same logic, plus the extra effect that the exercise rule is wrong.

DRAFT SENTENCES CURRENTLY IN THE TEX FILE (placeholders to rewrite in your own words, one per line)
  S1. If the stock has realised volatility $\sigma_{real}$ and the hedge uses $\sigma_h$, the
      discounted profit of the seller is, to leading order,
  S2. \begin{equation}\label{eq:gammapnl}  \varepsilon\approx\int_0^Te^{-rt}\,\tfrac12\,\Gamma_t
      S_t^2\,(\sigma_h^2-\sigma_{real}^2)\,dt, \end{equation} so the seller loses when the realised
      variance exceeds the variance used to price and hedge.
  S3. Table~\ref{tbl:hedgewrong} compares the simulated mean profit with the integral in
      Equation~\eqref{eq:gammapnl} evaluated along the same paths.
  S4. At a realised volatility of $55\%$ the simulation gives \$\WrongVolLoss\ and the formula gives
      \$\WrongVolTheory.
  S5. At realised volatilities of $25\%$ and $30\%$ the sign reverses and the seller gains by
      comparable amounts, so the profit is close to linear in the variance gap.
  S6. The size of the error is large compared with the discretisation error of
      Table~\ref{tbl:hedgefreq}: choosing the wrong volatility costs far more than trading weekly
      instead of daily.
  S7. Section~\ref{sec:misspec} repeats this experiment for the American put, where a wrong
      volatility also corrupts the exercise rule.

DERIVATION / LATEX MATERIAL (use as the skeleton of the displayed equations)
Self-financing hedge with vol $\sigma_h$: $dV=\Theta dt+\Delta dS+\tfrac12\Gamma(dS)^2$ and $\Theta+\tfrac12\sigma_h^2S^2\Gamma+rS\Delta-rV=0$.
Hedged P&L increment: $\tfrac12\Gamma S^2\big(\sigma_h^2\,dt-(dS/S)^2\big)$; with $(dS/S)^2=\sigma_{real}^2dt$ this gives Eq. (gammapnl) after discounting.

PITFALLS / DO NOT SAY
'Leading order' means the identity is exact only in continuous time with continuous hedging; the
table's small differences are discretisation and noise.
