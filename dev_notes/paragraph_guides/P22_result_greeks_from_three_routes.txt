DRAFT SENTENCES CURRENTLY IN THE TEX FILE (placeholders to rewrite in your own words, one per line)
  S1. Table~\ref{tbl:greeks} shows that all routes agree: $\Delta$ is \BSdelta\ from Black--Scholes,
      \TreeDelta\ from the tree and \MCdeltaPW\ from the pathwise Monte Carlo estimator.
  S2. The tree obtains $\Delta$ and $\Gamma$ for free from its first two layers, while vega and rho
      need a bump of the inputs.
  S3. For $\Gamma$ the tree gives \TreeGamma\ and the Monte Carlo estimator that combines the
      pathwise and likelihood-ratio ideas gives \MCgammaLR\ with standard error \MCgammaLRSE.
  S4. A bump-and-revalue estimator with common random numbers has a standard error of
      \MCgammaBumpSE, which is larger because the second difference divides the noise by the square
      of the bump size.
  S5. For vega the pathwise estimator has a smaller standard error than the likelihood-ratio
      estimator, because it differentiates the payoff, which is Lipschitz, instead of the density.
  S6. The likelihood-ratio method earns its place when the payoff is too irregular to differentiate,
      as for the second derivative in $\Gamma$.
  S7. Theta and rho agree between the closed form and the tree, and their size gives a feel for the
      risks: the call loses about \BSthetaDay\ dollars per calendar day at the money and gains about
      \BSrhoPct\ dollars for a one-point rise in the rate.
  S8. These are the numbers a desk would use to decide how often to re-price and how sensitive the
      book is to the input rate.

DERIVATION / LATEX MATERIAL (use as the skeleton of the displayed equations)
Pathwise delta: $\Delta=e^{-rT}\mathbb E[\mathbf 1_{S_T>K}S_T/S_0]$ (differentiate $(S_T-K)^+$ and use $\partial S_T/\partial S_0=S_T/S_0$).
Pathwise vega: $\mathcal V=e^{-rT}\mathbb E[\mathbf 1_{S_T>K}S_T(\sqrt TZ-\sigma T)]$.
LR delta: $e^{-rT}\mathbb E[(S_T-K)^+Z/(S_0\sigma\sqrt T)]$.  Gamma: $e^{-rT}\mathbb E[\mathbf 1_{S_T>K}\frac{S_T}{S_0^2}(\frac Z{\sigma\sqrt T}-1)]$.
