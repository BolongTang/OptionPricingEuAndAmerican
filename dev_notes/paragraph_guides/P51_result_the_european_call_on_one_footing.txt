  S1. Table~\ref{tbl:deskcall} shows that the vectorised CRR tree with $N=252$ reproduces the price

  S3. Plain Monte Carlo with $10^5$ samples has a standard error of \$\DeskMcPlainSE\ (the realised
      error is \$\DeskMcPlainErr) and takes \DeskMcPlainMs\ ms.
  S4. The control variate brings the standard error to \$\DeskMcCvSE\ at the same cost, and
      scrambled Sobol points with $2^{16}$ samples reach an error of \$\DeskSobolErr\ in
      \DeskSobolMs\ ms.
  S5. The Euler--Maruyama simulation with $252$ steps takes about \DeskMcEulerMs\ ms for the same
      $10^5$ samples, roughly two orders of magnitude more than the exact sampler, and has the same
      standard error.
  S6. For a European payoff on geometric Brownian motion the extra cost buys nothing, because the
      exact terminal law is known; the scheme earns its place when the dynamics are not solvable.
  S7. The order of the rows is stable across machines, but the absolute times are not and should be
      read as orders of magnitude.
