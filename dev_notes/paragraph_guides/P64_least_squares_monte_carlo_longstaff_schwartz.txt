ROLE IN THE ARGUMENT
Explains the algorithm used in Section 5.6, why it is biased low and what controls its accuracy.

CONTEXT THE READER ALREADY HAS / WHAT YOU MUST ESTABLISH
The reader has the dynamic programming principle for the American put (Section 5.1).

SENTENCE PLAN (write in this order; the numbers in brackets are sentence counts)
  S1-S2  [2 sentences]  FOCUS: Dynamic programming: with exercise dates t_1 < ... < t_n, V_n = payoff and V_j(x) = max{payoff(x), C_j(x)} with continuation value C_j(x) = e^{-r dt} E[V_{j+1}(S_{j+1}) | S_j = x].
  S3-S4  [2 sentences]  FOCUS: Regression: project the discounted realised cash flow on a polynomial basis of the spot using in-the-money paths: C_j(x) approx sum_k beta_k x^k.
  S5-S6  [2 sentences]  FOCUS: Algorithm: backward from the last date, exercise on a path if the payoff exceeds the fitted C_j and replace its cash flow by the payoff; the price is the average of the discounted cash flows, compared with the immediate payoff at t_0.
  S7-S8  [2 sentences]  FOCUS: Bias: the fitted rule is a feasible exercise policy, so its true value is a lower bound of the Bermudan value; using the same paths for fitting and valuation adds a small upward bias of order 1/M, so the estimate is low-biased up to noise; accuracy is controlled by the basis and the number of dates.

DRAFT SENTENCES CURRENTLY IN THE TEX FILE (placeholders to rewrite in your own words, one per line)
  S1. With exercise dates $t_1<\dots<t_n=T$ the value satisfies $V_n(x)=(K-x)^+$ and
      $V_j(x)=\max\{(K-x)^+,C_j(x)\}$ with the continuation value $C_j(x)=e^{-r\Delta t}\,\mathbb
      E[V_{j+1}(S_{j+1})\mid S_j=x]$.
  S2. This is the tree recursion with the one-step expectation replaced by a conditional expectation
      under the simulated law.
  S3. The conditional expectation is approximated by a regression of the discounted realised cash
      flow on a polynomial basis of the spot, $C_j(x)\approx\sum_{k=0}^{d}\beta_kx^k$, fitted on the
      paths that are in the money at $t_j$.
  S4. Only in-the-money paths are used because exercise is never optimal otherwise, and this
      concentrates the fit where the decision is made.
  S5. Working backward from the last date, a path exercises at $t_j$ if its payoff exceeds the
      fitted continuation value, and its cash flow is then replaced by the payoff.
  S6. The estimate is the average of the discounted cash flows, compared with the immediate payoff
      at the start.
  S7. Because the fitted rule is a feasible exercise policy, its expected payoff is a lower bound
      for the Bermudan value, which is why the estimator is biased low.
  S8. Using the same paths for the fit and for the valuation adds a small upward bias of order
      $1/M$, so in practice the estimate is low-biased up to noise, and the accuracy is controlled
      by the basis and by the number of exercise dates.
