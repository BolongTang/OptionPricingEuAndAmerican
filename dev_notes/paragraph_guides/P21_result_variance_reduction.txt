ROLE IN THE ARGUMENT
Shows how much accuracy can be bought per sample with antithetic variates, a control variate and
quasi-Monte Carlo, and where the advantage ends.

CONTEXT THE READER ALREADY HAS / WHAT YOU MUST ESTABLISH
Table tbl:vr and Figure vr compare four estimators with the same number of samples, repeated 40
times. All use the exact terminal sampler.

  S1-S2  [2 sentences]  FOCUS: Setup and variance ratios: at M = (macro VRM) samples the antithetic estimator reduces the variance by (macro VRanti), the control variate by (macro VRcv), scrambled Sobol by (macro VRsobol).
  S3-S4  [2 sentences]  FOCUS: Why the control variate works: S_T has a known mean S_0 e^{rT} and is strongly correlated with the payoff near the money; the coefficient is estimated from the same sample.
  S5-S6  [2 sentences]  FOCUS: Why Sobol is so strong here and where it stops: the integrand is a smooth function of one uniform variable; a path-dependent payoff has dimension N and the advantage shrinks.
  S7  [1 sentence]  FOCUS: Calibration of confidence intervals: 95 percent intervals from the plain estimator cover the true price in (macro MCcoverage) percent of 400 repetitions.
  S8  [1 sentence]  FOCUS: The classical error: simulating with the real-world drift 10 percent instead of r gives (macro WrongMuPrice) instead of (macro BSprice).

DRAFT SENTENCES CURRENTLY IN THE TEX FILE (placeholders to rewrite in your own words, one per line)
  S1. At $M=\VRM$ samples the antithetic estimator reduces the variance of the plain estimator by a
      factor of \VRanti\ and the control variate by a factor of \VRcv.
  S2. Scrambled Sobol quasi-Monte Carlo reduces it by a factor of \VRsobol, which is why it nearly
      disappears from the log--log plot of Figure~\ref{fig:vr}.
  S3. The control variate uses the discounted terminal price, whose expectation $S_0$ is known
      exactly and which is strongly correlated with the call payoff near the money.
  S4. The coefficient of the correction is estimated from the same sample, which introduces a
      negligible bias.
  S5. Quasi-Monte Carlo is so effective here because the integrand is a function of a single uniform
      variable, for which low-discrepancy points give an error close to $1/M$.
  S6. A path-dependent payoff depends on one random number per time step, and the advantage shrinks
      as this dimension grows unless a Brownian-bridge construction concentrates the variance in the
      first coordinates.
  S7. The standard errors are trustworthy: the 95\% intervals of the plain estimator cover the Black
      --Scholes price in \MCcoverage\% of 400 repetitions.
  S8. Simulating with a real-world drift of $10\%$ instead of $r$ would give a price of
      \$\WrongMuPrice\ instead of \$\BSprice, which is the classical error of pricing under the
      wrong measure.
