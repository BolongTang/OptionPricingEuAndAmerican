ROLE IN THE ARGUMENT
Turns the hedging claim of Section 5.1 into a number: the replicating portfolio reproduces the
option value at both successor nodes, as long as the holder has not exercised.

At every continuation node the hedge holds Delta shares and B = V - Delta S in the bond; one step
later the portfolio is worth Delta S' + B e^{r dt}.

DRAFT SENTENCES CURRENTLY IN THE TEX FILE (placeholders to rewrite in your own words, one per line)
  S1. We simulate $200$ paths of the baseline tree with $800$ steps and, at every date before
      exercise, compare the value of the replicating portfolio one step later with the option value
      at the node that was reached.
  S2. The portfolio holds $\Delta_{j,i}$ shares and $B=V_{j,i}-\Delta_{j,i}S_{j,i}$ in the bond.
  S3. The largest discrepancy over all hedge dates on all paths is \ReplError, which is rounding
      error.
  S4. Replication is therefore exact in the tree up to the optimal exercise time.
  S5. Inside the exercise region the identity fails by construction, because the holder is paid
      $K-S$, which exceeds the continuation value that the hedge replicates.
  S6. The excess is the seller's surplus when the holder exercises, and at the boundary nodes we
      sampled it is tiny, of the order of $0.0002$ in price units.
