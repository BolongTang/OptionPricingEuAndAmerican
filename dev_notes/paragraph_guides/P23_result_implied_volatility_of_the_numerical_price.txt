DRAFT SENTENCES CURRENTLY IN THE TEX FILE (placeholders to rewrite in your own words, one per line)
  S1. The implied volatility of an observed price is the $\hat\sigma$ that solves
      $C_{BS}(\hat\sigma)=C_{obs}$.
  S2. The root is unique because the Black--Scholes price is strictly increasing in $\sigma$, since
      vega is positive, and we find it with Brent's method.
  S3. Applied to the tree prices, the implied volatility differs from the true $37.73\%$ by
      \IVcrrHundred\ volatility points at $N=100$ (the tree underprices the call), and it inherits
      the saw-tooth of the price, as Figure~\ref{fig:iv} shows.
  S4. At $N=10{,}000$ the difference is below one hundredth of a volatility point.
  S5. For the best Monte Carlo price the 95\% interval of the price maps to an implied-volatility
      interval of \IVmcLo\% to \IVmcHi\%.
  S6. This is consistent with dividing the standard error of the price by vega, which is the
      quickest way to judge whether a simulation is accurate enough for a given tolerance in
      volatility.
  S7. Expressing numerical error in volatility points makes it directly comparable with the
      uncertainty in the volatility input itself, which is usually far larger.
