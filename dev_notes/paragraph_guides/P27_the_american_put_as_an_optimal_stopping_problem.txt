ROLE IN THE ARGUMENT
Defines the object of Part C and extracts the structural facts (bounds, regions, free boundary) that
all later numerical results are checked against.

CONTEXT THE READER ALREADY HAS / WHAT YOU MUST ESTABLISH
The reader knows the risk-neutral pricing rule for European claims. They need to see what changes
when the holder chooses the exercise date.

SENTENCE PLAN (write in this order; the numbers in brackets are sentence counts)
  S1-S2  [2 sentences]  FOCUS: Definition: V(t,x) = sup over stopping times tau in [t,T] of E[e^{-r(tau - t)} (K - S_tau)^+ | S_t = x] under Q; display.
  S3-S4  [2 sentences]  FOCUS: Two bounds: V >= (K - x)^+ (exercise now) and V >= V_Eur (the choice tau = T); hence the American price is never below the European price.
  S5-S6  [2 sentences]  FOCUS: Regions: continuation region C = {V > (K-x)^+} and stopping region D = {V = (K-x)^+} = {x <= b(t)} for a put; the free boundary b(t) must be found together with V; optimal rule tau* = inf{t : S_t <= b(t)}.
  S7  [1 sentence]  FOCUS: Why a recursion: the discounted value e^{-rt} V(t, S_t) is the smallest supermartingale dominating the discounted payoff (Snell envelope), and both numerical methods discretise this principle.

DRAFT SENTENCES CURRENTLY IN THE TEX FILE (placeholders to rewrite in your own words, one per line)
  S1. An American put may be exercised at any time $\tau\le T$ and then pays $(K-S_\tau)^+$.
  S2. The holder chooses $\tau$ using only the information available, a stopping time, so under
      $\mathbb Q$ the fair value is the optimal stopping problem \begin{equation}\label{eq:V}
      V(t,x)=\sup_{\tau\in[t,T]}\mathbb E\Big[e^{-r(\tau-t)}(K-S_\tau)^+\,\Big|\,S_t=x\Big].
      \end{equation}
  S3. Two bounds follow at once: $V(t,x)\ge(K-x)^+$, by exercising immediately, and $V(t,x)\ge
      V^{Eur}(t,x)$, by choosing $\tau=T$.
  S4. The American put is therefore never worth less than the European put or than its intrinsic
      value.
  S5. The state space splits into a continuation region $C=\{V>(K-x)^+\}$ and a stopping region
      $D=\{V=(K-x)^+\}$, which for a put has the form $\{x\le b(t)\}$.
  S6. The boundary $b(t)$ is a free boundary, because it is part of the unknown and has to be found
      together with $V$, and the optimal rule is $\tau^*=\inf\{t:S_t\le b(t)\}$.
  S7. Equivalently, the discounted value $e^{-rt}V(t,S_t)$ is the smallest $\mathbb
      Q$-supermartingale that dominates the discounted payoff, the Snell envelope, and both the tree
      and the PDE are discretisations of this principle.
