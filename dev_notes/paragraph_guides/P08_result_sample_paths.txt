DRAFT SENTENCES CURRENTLY IN THE TEX FILE (placeholders to rewrite in your own words, one per line)
  S1. Figure~\ref{fig:part1} shows that the walk with $N_2=10$ makes very few turns and looks like a
      polygon, whereas the walk with $N_2=1000$ is hard to tell from a Brownian path.
  S2. Between these two the number of turns and the roughness of the path grow steadily with $N_2$.
  S3. The maxima and minima of the walks are comparable for every $N_2$, because the variance at
      $t=1$ is equal to $1$ whatever the step size.
  S4. What changes with $N_2$ is the fine structure of the path, not its overall range.
  S5. Neither object has a derivative, so the Brownian paths are not smoother than the walks: it is
      the walk that becomes Brownian as $N_2$ grows.
  S6. This is the point of Donsker's theorem.
  S7. A picture is suggestive but not a measurement, so the next three paragraphs quantify the
      convergence.
