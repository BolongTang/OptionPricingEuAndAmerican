ROLE IN THE ARGUMENT

and explains why this forces Ito calculus.

SENTENCE PLAN (write in this order; the numbers in brackets are sentence counts)
  S1-S2  [2 sentences]  FOCUS: Quadratic variation: for Brownian paths it is close to 1 with a standard deviation that shrinks like N^{-1/2} (state the value at N = 10,000); for the walk it is exactly 1.
  S3-S4  [2 sentences]  FOCUS: Total variation: it grows like sqrt(2N/pi) for Brownian paths (value at N = 10,000 against the theoretical value) and like sqrt(N) for the walk.
  S5-S6  [2 sentences]  FOCUS: Consequence for calculus: infinite total variation excludes Riemann-Stieltjes integrals, finite quadratic variation produces the Ito correction, which is the origin of the term sigma^2/2 in the solution of GBM.
  S7  [1 sentence]  FOCUS: Consequence for hedging: the roughness is the reason a discretely rebalanced hedge keeps a residual error (Section on hedging).

DRAFT SENTENCES CURRENTLY IN THE TEX FILE (placeholders to rewrite in your own words, one per line)
  S1. The quadratic variation $\sum_k(\Delta W_k)^2$ of a Brownian path on $[0,1]$ is close to $1$
      and its spread shrinks as $N$ grows; at $N=10{,}000$ the mean is \QVBMtenk.
  S2. For the scaled walk it equals $1$ exactly, because every squared step is $T/N$.
  S3. The total variation $\sum_k|\Delta W_k|$ does not converge: at $N=10{,}000$ it is \TVBMtenk\
      for the Brownian paths, which matches the theoretical value \TVtheorytenk\ to the digits
      shown, and it equals $\sqrt N=100$ for the walk.
  S4. Table~\ref{tbl:variation} shows the same $\sqrt N$ growth at every $N$.
  S5. Infinite total variation means that $\int\sigma S\,dW$ cannot be defined path by path as an
      ordinary integral, which is why It\^o calculus is needed.
  S6. Finite and non-zero quadratic variation is what produces the correction $-\tfrac12\sigma^2$ in
      the drift of $\ln S_t$ in Section~\ref{sec:B-gbm}.
  S7. The same roughness explains why a hedge that is rebalanced only at discrete dates keeps a
      residual error (Section~\ref{sec:hedge}).
