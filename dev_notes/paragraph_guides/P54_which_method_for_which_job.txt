SENTENCE PLAN (write in this order; the numbers in brackets are sentence counts)
  S1-S2  [2 sentences]  FOCUS: A single vanilla European option: the closed form if it exists; otherwise a Leisen-Reimer tree or smoothing plus Richardson; Monte Carlo only as a cross-check.
  S3-S4  [2 sentences]  FOCUS: A single-underlying American option: a CRR tree with Richardson extrapolation for prices, boundary and hedges, or the PDE solver when high accuracy is wanted; the approximation of Barone-Adesi-Whaley for screening.
  S5-S6  [2 sentences]  FOCUS: Path-dependent or multi-asset payoffs: Monte Carlo with variance reduction, plus regression for early exercise; the tree and the grid are out because the cost grows exponentially with the dimension.
  S7-S8  [2 sentences]  FOCUS: Sensitivities and risk: Greeks from the same lattice or grid where possible, pathwise estimators in Monte Carlo, and a stress of the volatility input, since the volatility error costs more than any numerical error in this report.

DRAFT SENTENCES CURRENTLY IN THE TEX FILE (placeholders to rewrite in your own words, one per line)
  S1. For a vanilla European option the closed form is the answer when it exists, and otherwise a
      Leisen--Reimer tree or a smoothed and extrapolated CRR tree is faster and more accurate than
      any simulation.
  S2. Monte Carlo serves there only as an independent cross-check.
  S3. For an American option on one underlying a CRR tree with Richardson extrapolation delivers the
      price, the exercise boundary and the hedge in tens of milliseconds, and a PDE solver is the
      better choice when high accuracy is needed.
  S4. The Barone--Adesi--Whaley formula is good enough for screening and for initialising a root-
      finder.
  S5. For path-dependent payoffs or several underlyings, Monte Carlo with variance reduction is the
      default, with a regression step such as Longstaff--Schwartz if early exercise is allowed.
  S6. The cost of a tree or a grid grows exponentially with the dimension and rules them out beyond
      two or three underlyings.
  S7. Sensitivities come from the same lattice or grid as the price where possible, and from
      pathwise or likelihood-ratio estimators in Monte Carlo.
  S8. A stress of the volatility input is more informative than any refinement of the numerics,
      because in both parts of this report the effect of a wrong volatility dwarfs the numerical
      error of a reasonable method.
