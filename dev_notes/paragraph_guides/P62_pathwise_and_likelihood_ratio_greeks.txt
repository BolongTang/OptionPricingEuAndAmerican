PARAGRAPH P62: Pathwise and likelihood-ratio Greeks

ROLE IN THE ARGUMENT
Derives the Monte Carlo estimators used in Table greeks, with the reason each one works and where
each one fails.

CONTEXT THE READER ALREADY HAS / WHAT YOU MUST ESTABLISH
The reader knows Monte Carlo pricing and the lognormal sampler S_T = S_0 exp((r - sigma^2/2)T +
sigma sqrt(T) Z).

SENTENCE PLAN (write in this order; the numbers in brackets are sentence counts)
  S1-S2  [2 sentences]  FOCUS: Problem: estimate the derivative with respect to a parameter theta of C(theta) = e^{-rT} E[f(S_T(theta))]; two ideas: differentiate the path, or differentiate the density.
  S3-S4  [2 sentences]  FOCUS: Pathwise: if f is Lipschitz and S_T is differentiable in theta, then d/d theta E f(S_T) = E[f'(S_T) d S_T/d theta]; for the call f'(s) = 1{s > K}.
  S5-S6  [2 sentences]  FOCUS: Delta and vega: dS_T/dS_0 = S_T/S_0 and dS_T/d sigma = S_T (sqrt(T) Z - sigma T); the estimators are e^{-rT} 1{S_T > K} S_T/S_0 and e^{-rT} 1{S_T > K} S_T (sqrt(T) Z - sigma T).
  S7-S8  [2 sentences]  FOCUS: Likelihood ratio: write E f = integral f(s) g(s; theta) ds and differentiate g, so d/d theta E f = E[f(S_T) d ln g/d theta]; for the lognormal density the score for the spot is Z/(S_0 sigma sqrt(T)), giving Delta = e^{-rT} E[(S_T - K)^+ Z/(S_0 sigma sqrt(T))].
  S9  [1 sentence]  FOCUS: Gamma: differentiate the pathwise delta integrand by the likelihood-ratio method, giving e^{-rT} E[1{S_T > K} (S_T/S_0^2)(Z/(sigma sqrt(T)) - 1)]; likelihood ratio has larger variance for smooth payoffs and is indispensable for discontinuous ones.

DRAFT SENTENCES CURRENTLY IN THE TEX FILE (placeholders to rewrite in your own words, one per line)
  S1. We want $\partial_\theta C(\theta)$ for $C(\theta)=e^{-rT}\mathbb E[f(S_T(\theta))]$, where
      $\theta$ is the spot, the volatility or the rate.
  S2. There are two ways to move the derivative inside: differentiate the sample path, or
      differentiate the density of $S_T$.
  S3. The pathwise method requires $f$ to be Lipschitz and $S_T$ to be differentiable in $\theta$,
      and then $\partial_\theta\mathbb E f(S_T)=\mathbb E[f'(S_T)\,\partial_\theta S_T]$.
  S4. For the call $f'(s)=\mathbf 1_{\{s>K\}}$, which exists except at a single point that has
      probability zero.
  S5. Since $\partial S_T/\partial S_0=S_T/S_0$ and $\partial S_T/\partial\sigma=S_T(\sqrt TZ-\sigma
      T)$, the pathwise estimators are
  S6. \begin{equation}  \hat\Delta=e^{-rT}\mathbf 1_{\{S_T>K\}}\frac{S_T}{S_0},\qquad \hat{\mathcal
      V}=e^{-rT}\mathbf 1_{\{S_T>K\}}\,S_T\big(\sqrt TZ-\sigma T\big). \end{equation}
  S7. The likelihood-ratio method writes $\mathbb Ef(S_T)=\int f(s)\,g(s;\theta)\,ds$ and
      differentiates the density, $\partial_\theta\mathbb Ef(S_T)=\mathbb
      E[f(S_T)\,\partial_\theta\ln g(S_T;\theta)]$, which needs no smoothness of $f$.
  S8. For the lognormal density the score with respect to the spot is $Z/(S_0\sigma\sqrt T)$, so
      $\Delta=e^{-rT}\mathbb E\big[(S_T-K)^+Z/(S_0\sigma\sqrt T)\big]$.
  S9. For the second derivative we apply the likelihood-ratio idea to the pathwise delta, which
      gives $\Gamma=e^{-rT}\mathbb E\big[\mathbf
      1_{\{S_T>K\}}\tfrac{S_T}{S_0^2}\big(\tfrac{Z}{\sigma\sqrt T}-1\big)\big]$, and in general the
      likelihood-ratio estimators have larger variance for smooth payoffs but remain valid for
      discontinuous ones.

DERIVATION / LATEX MATERIAL (use as the skeleton of the displayed equations)
Gamma via LR on the pathwise delta: $\Delta=\partial_{S_0}\mathbb E[F]$, $F=e^{-rT}\mathbf 1_{\{S_T>K\}}S_T/S_0$. $\partial_{S_0}\mathbb E[F]=\mathbb E[\partial_{S_0}F+F\cdot Z/(S_0\sigma\sqrt T)]$ with $\partial_{S_0}F=-e^{-rT}\mathbf 1_{\{S_T>K\}}S_T/S_0^2$ (the indicator's derivative is handled by the score term).

PITFALLS / DO NOT SAY
The Gamma derivation above is the standard mixed estimator; its check is numerical (Table greeks
agrees with Black-Scholes to the shown digits).
