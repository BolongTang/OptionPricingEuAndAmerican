DRAFT SENTENCES CURRENTLY IN THE TEX FILE (placeholders to rewrite in your own words, one per line)
  S1. Figure~\ref{fig:workprecision} plots the error of each method against the CPU time it uses, on
      logarithmic axes.
  S2. The tree errors fall with slope about $-1$ (CRR) or $-2$ (Leisen--Reimer) in the number of
      steps, the Monte Carlo standard errors fall with slope $-\tfrac12$ in the time, and the Sobol
      points have a much steeper slope because the integral is one-dimensional.
  S3. To bring the error below \$\WPtol, plain CRR needs $N=\WPcrrN$ steps and \WPcrrMs\ ms, CRR
      with smoothing and Richardson needs $N=\WPsmN$ and \WPsmMs\ ms, and the Leisen--Reimer tree
      needs $N=\WPlrN$ and \WPlrMs\ ms.
  S4. The gap between the first and the last is therefore a factor of several hundred in time.
  S5. Plain Monte Carlo needs about $\WPmcM$ samples for a standard error of \$\WPtol, which is
      about \WPmcSec\ seconds by extrapolation, and the control variate needs about \WPcvSec\
      seconds.
  S6. Scrambled Sobol points reach the same accuracy with \WPsobolM\ points in a few milliseconds.
  S7. This ranking is specific to a one-dimensional European payoff, and it reverses for many
      underlyings or for path-dependent payoffs, where lattices become infeasible and Monte Carlo
      keeps its rate.
