  S1. Table~\ref{tbl:amlongfine} uses $M_{bin}=\max(800,8T)$ steps, so that $\Delta t\le0.125$, and
      finds the boundary at date zero by bisection on the decision at the root, which determines
      $b(0;T)$ to the accuracy of the bisection instead of to a grid of step $0.1$.
  S2. The price and the boundary now increase and decrease monotonically with $T$ up to $T=200$.
  S3. For $T=500$ and $T=1000$ the rows coincide, with $V=\LongVthousand$ and $b=\LongBthousand$, so
      the gap of \LongGapThousand\ to the perpetual value is no longer a maturity effect: it is a
      lattice effect.
  S4. At $T=1000$ the effect of maturity is of order $e^{-40}$.
  S5. Table~\ref{tbl:amlonglattice} refines the lattice at $T=500$: the gap to $2.5$ falls from
      \LatVgapCoarse\ to \LatVgap\ and the boundary comes down from \LatBcoarse\ to \LatBfine\ as
      $\Delta t$ goes from $0.25$ to $0.03$.
  S6. The price gap is not monotone in $M_{bin}$ (it oscillates with the position of the nodes
      relative to $S^*$), whereas the boundary error decreases steadily.
  S7. After subtracting the lattice floor of \LongFloor, the distance $V_\infty(S_0)-V(0,S_0)$
      decays roughly exponentially in $T$, with a fitted rate of \LongDecay\ per year between $T=5$
      and $T=100$ (Figure~\ref{fig:longT}).
  S8. This rate is an empirical fit, not a derived constant, and it applies to this parameter set
      only.
