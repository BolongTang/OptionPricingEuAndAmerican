ROLE IN THE ARGUMENT

CONTEXT THE READER ALREADY HAS / WHAT YOU MUST ESTABLISH
The reflection principle: for Brownian motion P(max_{s<=1} W_s >= a) = 2(1 - Phi(a)); for the simple
symmetric walk the identity holds exactly on the lattice. Figure fig:runmax plots both.

  S3-S4  [2 sentences]  FOCUS: Result: Figure fig:runmax; the walk's curve lies below Brownian motion and the largest gap is (macro MaxGapTen) at N2 = 10 and (macro MaxGapTwoHundred) at N2 = 200.

  S7  [1 sentence]  FOCUS: Practical consequence: discretely monitored barriers are crossed less often than continuously monitored ones; a tree needs a barrier shift (of order 0.58 sigma sqrt(dt)) to compensate.

DRAFT SENTENCES CURRENTLY IN THE TEX FILE (placeholders to rewrite in your own words, one per line)
  S1. For a Brownian motion the reflection principle gives $\mathbb P(\max_{s\le1}W_s\ge
      a)=2\,(1-\Phi(a))$.
  S2. For the simple symmetric walk the same argument works exactly on the lattice and gives
      $\mathbb P(\max_{n\le N}S_n\ge k)=\mathbb P(S_N\ge k)+\mathbb P(S_N>k)$.
  S3. Figure~\ref{fig:runmax} shows that the survival function of the walk's maximum lies below the
      Brownian one, with a largest gap of \MaxGapTen\ at $N_2=10$ and \MaxGapTwoHundred\ at
      $N_2=200$.
  S4. The gap shrinks only like $N_2^{-1/2}$, the same slow rate as for the terminal law.
  S5. A discrete walk can cross a level only at a monitoring date, so it misses the excursions that
      happen between dates and its maximum is biased downwards.
  S6. The same bias appears whenever a path-dependent payoff is computed on a lattice or on a
      simulated grid.
  S7. For a barrier option this means that a tree or a simulation overstates the value of a knock-
      out and understates that of a knock-in unless the barrier is shifted by an amount of order
      $0.58\,\sigma\sqrt{\Delta t}$.

DERIVATION / LATEX MATERIAL (use as the skeleton of the displayed equations)
Reflection (BM): $\mathbb P(\max_{s\le1}W_s\ge a,\ W_1<a)=\mathbb P(W_1>a)\Rightarrow\mathbb P(\max\ge a)=2\mathbb P(W_1\ge a)$.
Lattice: for integer level $k\ge1$, $\mathbb P(\max_{n\le N}S_n\ge k)=\mathbb P(S_N\ge k)+\mathbb P(S_N>k)$.
