DRAFT SENTENCES CURRENTLY IN THE TEX FILE (placeholders to rewrite in your own words, one per line)
  S1. A continuous dividend yield $q$ lowers the risk-neutral drift of the stock to $r-q$, so the
      tree uses $p=(e^{(r-q)\Delta t}-d)/(u-d)$ while discounting stays at $r$.
  S2. The subclass \texttt{CRRTreeGen} changes only this probability and the payoff, so the
      boundary, the hedge and the stopping rule are inherited from \texttt{CRRTree}.
  S3. With $q=0$ the tree never exercises the American call early, and its price equals the European
      price on the same lattice.
  S4. This is the classical result that early exercise of a call is never optimal without dividends.
  S5. Table~\ref{tbl:div} shows that the premium grows with the yield, from zero to \DivPremSix\ at
      $q=6\%$.
  S6. The exercise region of a call is $\{x\ge b(t)\}$ with $b(t)$ above the strike, and
      Figure~\ref{fig:divboundary} shows that the boundary comes down as the yield rises.
  S7. The intuition is that waiting costs the dividends $qS$ per year and earns interest $rK$ on the
      deferred strike payment, so early exercise becomes attractive when the yield is large relative
      to the interest rate.
