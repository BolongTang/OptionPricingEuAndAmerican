  S5-S6  [2 sentences]  FOCUS: The hedge and the stopping time: Delta_{j,i} = (V_{j+1,i+1} - V_{j+1,i})/(S_{j+1,i+1} - S_{j+1,i}); the optimal stopping time of a simulated path is the first date at which it lies in the exercise region.

DRAFT SENTENCES CURRENTLY IN THE TEX FILE (placeholders to rewrite in your own words, one per line)
  S1. With $\Delta t=T/M_{bin}$, the CRR parameters of Section~\ref{sec:B} and
      $V_{M,i}=(K-S_{M,i})^+$, the American put is priced by
  S2. \begin{equation}\label{eq:crr}  V_{j,i}=\max\Big\{(K-S_{j,i})^+,\;e^{-r\Delta
      t}\big(p\,V_{j+1,i+1}+(1-p)\,V_{j+1,i}\big)\Big\}, \end{equation} where the first term is the
      value of exercising now and the second the value of waiting.
  S3. The exercise boundary $b(t_j)$ is the largest node at which exercising is strictly better than
      waiting, with a tolerance of $10^{-12}$ so that ties are resolved in favour of waiting.
  S4. The tolerance matters for $r=0$, where exercise and continuation coincide deep in the money,
      and because the nodes of a CRR tree alternate between two grids on odd and even layers the
      boundary is plotted on even layers only.
  S5. The hedge at node $(j,i)$ is the ratio
      $\Delta_{j,i}=\frac{V_{j+1,i+1}-V_{j+1,i}}{S_{j+1,i+1}-S_{j+1,i}}$ of the changes in option
      value and stock price over one step.
  S6. The optimal stopping time of a simulated path is the first date at which the path lies in the
      exercise region, and it is held to maturity if it never does.
  S7. The class \texttt{CRRTree} extends \texttt{CRRBinomialModel} of Section~\ref{sec:B}: it adds
      the American recursion, a vectorised simulation of paths and the stopping rule, and inherits
      the lattice.
  S8. The base class counts maturity in trading days, so the subclass passes
      \texttt{days=round(252*T)}, and each layer of the recursion is evaluated with array
      operations.
