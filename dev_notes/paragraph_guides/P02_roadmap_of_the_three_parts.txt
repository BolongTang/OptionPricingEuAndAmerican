DRAFT SENTENCES CURRENTLY IN THE TEX FILE (placeholders to rewrite in your own words, one per line)
  S1. Section~\ref{sec:A} shows that a scaled symmetric random walk converges to Brownian motion and
      measures the convergence in three ways: quadratic and total variation, an exact Kolmogorov
      distance at the terminal time, and the law of the running maximum.
  S2. This is the bridge between the discrete and the continuous language, because the binomial tree
      is a random walk with drift.
  S3. Section~\ref{sec:B} prices a one-year at-the-money call on NVIDIA with the Cox--Ross--
      Rubinstein (CRR) tree and with Monte Carlo simulation of geometric Brownian motion, and
      benchmarks both against Black--Scholes.
  S4. It then asks what a practitioner would ask next: how to make each method more accurate, how to
      compute sensitivities, how to read the price as an implied volatility, and how well the option
      can be hedged in discrete time.
  S5. Section~\ref{sec:C} turns to the American put: the optimal stopping problem and its
      variational PDE, the closed-form perpetual put, the CRR tree with its exercise boundary and
      hedge, and independent checks by a finite-difference solver, least-squares Monte Carlo and a
      quasi-analytic approximation.
  S6. It closes with the early-exercise premium, dividends, an American put on the NVIDIA inputs,
      and the profit and loss of the buyer under the pricing measure and under the real-world
      measure.
  S7. Section~\ref{sec:compare} compares every method on accuracy and run time, and
      Section~\ref{sec:concl} states the conclusions and their limits.

HUMANISING NOTES FOR THIS TYPE OF PARAGRAPH
Write as the author talking to a reader who knows basic probability but not this project. Open with
the concrete problem, not with a definition. Avoid lists of three adjectives and avoid sentences
that only announce what the next sentence will say.
General: vary sentence length (mix a 6-word sentence with a 35-word one), keep one idea per
sentence, put the hedge where the doubt actually is, and delete any sentence you cannot defend with
a number, an equation or a reference. If the paragraph ends up outside 6-9 sentences, split or merge
it rather than padding.
