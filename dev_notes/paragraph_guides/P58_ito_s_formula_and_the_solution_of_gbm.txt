SENTENCE PLAN (write in this order; the numbers in brackets are sentence counts)
  S1  [1 sentence]  FOCUS: The multiplication table: (dt)^2 = 0, dt dW = 0, (dW)^2 = dt; the last is the quadratic variation of Section 3.
  S2-S3  [2 sentences]  FOCUS: Ito's formula for f(S): df = f' dS + (1/2) f'' (dS)^2, with (dS)^2 = sigma^2 S^2 dt.
  S4-S5  [2 sentences]  FOCUS: For f = ln: f' = 1/S, f'' = -1/S^2, so d ln S = (mu - sigma^2/2) dt + sigma dW; integrate and exponentiate.
  S6  [1 sentence]  FOCUS: Check: apply Ito to g(t,W) = S_0 exp((mu - sigma^2/2) t + sigma W) and recover dS = mu S dt + sigma S dW.
  S7-S8  [2 sentences]  FOCUS: Moments: ln S_t is normal with mean ln S_0 + (mu - sigma^2/2)t and variance sigma^2 t; E[S_t] = S_0 e^{mu t} and Var[S_t] = S_0^2 e^{2 mu t}(e^{sigma^2 t} - 1), from E[e^{sigma W_t}] = e^{sigma^2 t/2}.

DRAFT SENTENCES CURRENTLY IN THE TEX FILE (placeholders to rewrite in your own words, one per line)
  S1. The rules of It\^o calculus follow from the quadratic variation of Brownian motion:
      $(dt)^2=0$, $dt\,dW=0$ and $(dW)^2=dt$.
  S2. For a twice differentiable $f$ and $dS=\mu S\,dt+\sigma S\,dW$ it gives, using
      $(dS)^2=\sigma^2S^2\,dt$,
  S3. \begin{equation}  df(S_t)=f'(S_t)\,dS_t+\tfrac12f''(S_t)\,\sigma^2S_t^2\,dt . \end{equation}
  S4. With $f=\ln$ one has $f'=1/S$ and $f''=-1/S^2$, so $d\ln
      S_t=(\mu-\tfrac12\sigma^2)\,dt+\sigma\,dW_t$.
  S5. Integrating from $0$ to $t$ and exponentiating gives
      $S_t=S_0\exp\big((\mu-\tfrac12\sigma^2)t+\sigma W_t\big)$.
  S6. To check this, apply It\^o's formula to $g(t,W)=S_0e^{(\mu-\frac12\sigma^2)t+\sigma W}$:
      $dg=(\mu-\tfrac12\sigma^2)g\,dt+\sigma g\,dW+\tfrac12\sigma^2g\,dt=\mu g\,dt+\sigma g\,dW$.
  S7. Hence $\ln S_t$ is normal with mean $\ln S_0+(\mu-\tfrac12\sigma^2)t$ and variance
      $\sigma^2t$, and since $\mathbb E[e^{\sigma W_t}]=e^{\sigma^2t/2}$ one gets $\mathbb
      E[S_t]=S_0e^{\mu t}$ and $\operatorname{Var}[S_t]=S_0^2e^{2\mu t}\big(e^{\sigma^2t}-1\big)$.
  S8. The variance is what sets the size of the Monte Carlo standard errors of Section~\ref{sec:B}.
