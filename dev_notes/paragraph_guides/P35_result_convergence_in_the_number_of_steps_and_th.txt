DRAFT SENTENCES CURRENTLY IN THE TEX FILE (placeholders to rewrite in your own words, one per line)
  S1. Table~\ref{tbl:amconv} shows that the tree price increases from $0.7088$ at $M_{bin}=50$ to
      \AmVeight\ at $M_{bin}=800$ and \AmV\ at $M_{bin}=1600$.
  S2. The successive changes halve each time $M_{bin}$ doubles, so the ratio in the fourth column
      stays close to $2$.
  S3. A ratio of two is the signature of first-order convergence, $O(1/M_{bin})$, as for the
      European tree.
  S4. Extrapolating, the limit is close to $0.7111$, a value confirmed by independent methods in
      Section~\ref{sec:validators}.
  S5. The exercise boundary $b(t;1)$ in Figure~\ref{fig:b} rises from about \AmBearly\ near $t=0$ to
      the strike at maturity, and the curves for the six step counts lie on top of each other.
  S6. At $t=0.5$ it equals \AmBhalf\ for the finest tree, and the column of Table~\ref{tbl:amconv}
      varies only by about one lattice spacing between step counts.
  S7. With six months left the holder exercises once the stock has fallen about a fifth below the
      strike, and close to maturity the boundary approaches $K$.
  S8. This shape reflects the trade-off behind the problem: waiting forgoes interest on the strike,
      which is worth more than the remaining optionality when the option is deep in the money.
