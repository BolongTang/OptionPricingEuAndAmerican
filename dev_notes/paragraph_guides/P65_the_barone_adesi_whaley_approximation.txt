ROLE IN THE ARGUMENT
Sketches the idea behind a formula that is quoted in Section 5.6 without derivation, enough for a
reader to see where its error comes from.

CONTEXT THE READER ALREADY HAS / WHAT YOU MUST ESTABLISH
The reader has the Black-Scholes PDE and the notion of an early-exercise premium.

SENTENCE PLAN (write in this order; the numbers in brackets are sentence counts)
  S1-S2  [2 sentences]  FOCUS: Idea: write P_A = P_E + eps; both P_A and P_E satisfy the Black-Scholes PDE in the continuation region, hence so does eps.
  S3-S4  [2 sentences]  FOCUS: Approximation: take eps = kappa(T) f(S) with kappa = 1 - e^{-rT}; neglecting the term in d f/d kappa turns the PDE into an Euler ODE S^2 f'' + N S f' - (M/kappa) f = 0 with M = 2r/sigma^2 and N = 2(r-q)/sigma^2.
  S5-S6  [2 sentences]  FOCUS: Boundary conditions: value matching and smooth fit at the critical price S** give the equation for S** and the constant A_1; display the result.
  S7-S8  [2 sentences]  FOCUS: Where the error comes from: the neglected term is exactly zero only for kappa = constant, so the error grows with maturity; the formula has a constant boundary, while the true boundary depends on time.

DRAFT SENTENCES CURRENTLY IN THE TEX FILE (placeholders to rewrite in your own words, one per line)
  S1. Write the American put as $P_A=P_E+\varepsilon$, the European put plus the early-exercise
      premium.
  S2. Both $P_A$ and $P_E$ satisfy the Black--Scholes PDE in the continuation region, hence so does
      $\varepsilon$.
  S3. Barone-Adesi and Whaley set $\varepsilon=\kappa(T)f(S)$ with $\kappa=1-e^{-rT}$ and neglect a
      term that is small, which turns the PDE into an Euler equation
      $S^2f''+NSf'-\frac{M}{\kappa}f=0$ with $M=2r/\sigma^2$ and $N=2(r-q)/\sigma^2$.
  S4. Its decaying solution is a power of the spot, $f=A_1S^{q_1}$, where
      $q_1=\tfrac12\big[-(N-1)-\sqrt{(N-1)^2+4M/\kappa}\big]<0$.
  S5. Value matching and smooth fit at the critical price $S^{**}$ then fix both $S^{**}$ and $A_1$.
  S6. The result is \begin{equation} P_A(S)=P_E(S)+A_1\Big(\frac S{S^{**}}\Big)^{q_1}\
      (S>S^{**}),\qquad A_1=-\frac{S^{**}}{q_1}\Big[1-e^{(b-r)T}\Phi\big(-d_1(S^{**})\big)\Big],
      \end{equation} where $S^{**}$ solves
      $K-S^{**}=P_E(S^{**})-\big[1-e^{(b-r)T}\Phi(-d_1(S^{**}))\big]S^{**}/q_1$ and $b=r-q$.
  S7. The neglected term vanishes only if $\kappa$ were constant in time, so the approximation
      degrades as the maturity grows, as Table~\ref{tbl:baw} shows.
  S8. The formula also has a single critical price, whereas the true boundary depends on time, which
      is why it is best at short and medium maturities.
